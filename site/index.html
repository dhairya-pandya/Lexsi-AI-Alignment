The finding

Same model, two readings

Take Tiny Aya Fire, defend it with the usual SafeTune methods (Lisa, Vaccine, Booster, RepNoise), then run a poisoned fine-tune in Bengali. Evaluate in both languages.

English
0–4%
Bengali
59–61%

The defense is language-local. It holds exactly where it was trained and tested, and nowhere else. Adding Bengali safety data barely helps (harm 0.57 to 0.46): the attack overwrites the safety you add. This is a tamper-resistance problem, not a coverage gap. Harm is a HarmBench / Qwen3Guard unsafe rate; 95% bootstrap CIs, one seed.

Strip the top-k singular directions of the attack's weight update per matrix. Post-hoc, training-free. Bengali harm 50% → 14% at k=1 with MMLU preserved. The headline defense.

Experiment A · the repair

The damage is low-rank, so strip it out

The attack's weight change dW is nearly rank-1. Removing its top singular directions per matrix removes the harmful write and keeps the diffuse benign gain. No retraining.

What it means

The takeaways

01

English-only evaluation certifies unsafe models

A defended model can look robust in English and answer 59–61% of harmful prompts in Bengali. If you only test in English, you never see it.

02

The gap is tamper, not coverage

Adding target-language safety data barely helps. The attack overwrites the safety you add. Fixing it means resisting the write, not patching the blind spot.

03

The damage is low-rank and repairable

The attack concentrates in ~2 singular directions. Project them out and Bengali harm drops 50% → 14% with capability intact. Cheap, training-free, post-hoc.

04

Repair beats prevention

The training-time counterpart, subspace anchoring, did not close the gap and sometimes made it worse. The actionable defense is post-hoc repair of the low-rank write.

The gap, and the repair

What an English-only evaluation misses on Tiny Aya Fire, and the post-hoc fix. Every harm number is a HarmBench / Qwen3Guard unsafe rate with a 95% bootstrap CI; one seed.

Attack: Bengali SFT, p=0.2

01

Same model, two readings

A defended model, after a Bengali fine-tuning attack. In English it refuses almost everything; in Bengali it answers most harmful prompts. An English-only eval reports only the left bar.
02

The repair: remove the attack's top weight directions

The attack's weight change dW = W_post − W_base is nearly rank-1. Removing the top-k singular directions per attention/MLP matrix strips the harmful write and keeps the diffuse benign gain. Bengali harm falls from 50% to 14% at the first direction; English harm is already low.
Bengali harm English harm 95% CI
Capability holds. MMLU is 0.55 before the repair and 0.56 at k=1 (0.50–0.58 across all k) — the fix is not degeneration. Over-refusal rises modestly, 5% to 16%.
03

Prevention does not work — repair does

The training-time alternative: anchor the refusal subspace during fine-tuning so the attack cannot land. It fails. Anchoring the English direction leaves Bengali harm unchanged; anchoring all three languages makes it worse.
Over-constraining along machine-translated non-English refusal directions appears to fight the task objective. The post-hoc repair in panel 02 is what closes the gap.
